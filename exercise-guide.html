Cycle-Aware Movement

Exercise that flows with you

Your energy, strength, and recovery shift across your cycle. Train in harmony — not against — your hormones. i

water_drop Menstrual Phase (Days 1–5)

Estrogen and progesterone are at their lowest. Your body is shedding the uterine lining and conserving energy. This is a time for gentle, restorative movement. i

  • Recommended: Light walking, restorative yoga, stretching, gentle swimming
  • Intensity: Low (2–4 METs)
  • Duration: 15–30 minutes, 3–4 days this week
  • Avoid: High-intensity interval training (HIIT), heavy lifting, inversion poses if flow is heavy
TCM Wisdom: During menstruation, avoid cold drinks and raw foods — they can cause "cold stagnation" in the uterus, worsening cramps. Warm ginger tea and cooked foods support smooth flow. i

spa Follicular Phase (Days 6–13)

Estrogen is rising steadily, boosting energy, mood, and muscle recovery. This is your strength-building phase — you'll feel stronger and recover faster. i

  • Recommended: Strength training, running, cycling, dance, HIIT (later in phase)
  • Intensity: Moderate-High (5–9 METs)
  • Duration: 30–45 minutes, 4–5 days this week
  • Focus: Progressive overload — this is the best time to increase weights or try new exercises

auto_awesome Ovulatory Phase (Days 14–16)

Estrogen peaks and testosterone rises, giving you a natural energy surge. This is your peak performance window — great for personal records and high-intensity efforts. i

  • Recommended: HIIT, sprint intervals, heavy lifting, competitive sports, long runs
  • Intensity: High-Very High (7–10 METs)
  • Duration: 30–60 minutes, 3–4 days this window
  • Tip: Schedule your hardest workouts around ovulation — you'll have more power and better recovery
Note: Some women experience mild ovulation pain ("mittelschmerz") on one side. If this is uncomfortable, scale back to moderate intensity for that day.

nightlight Luteal Phase (Days 17–28)

Progesterone rises then falls, which can increase fatigue, body temperature, and PMS symptoms. Energy gradually declines. This is a time for moderate, steady movement with extra recovery. i

  • Recommended: Moderate cardio, Pilates, yoga, swimming, light strength training
  • Intensity: Moderate (4–6 METs) — scale back as you approach your period
  • Duration: 25–40 minutes, 3–4 days this week
  • Focus: Consistency over intensity. Movement helps reduce PMS symptoms and bloating
TCM Wisdom: In the luteal phase, avoid excessive cold foods and drinks. If you experience breast tenderness or irritability, gentle liver-meridian stretches (side bends, twists) can help move "Liver Qi stagnation." i

️ Acupressure for Cramps & Fatigue

  • SP6 (Sanyinjiao): 4 finger-widths above the inner ankle bone. Massage for 1–2 minutes each side. Relieves menstrual cramps, regulates cycles. i
  • LV3 (Taichong): Between the big toe and second toe, 2 finger-widths up from the web. Relieves PMS irritability and breast tenderness.
  • ST36 (Zusanli): 4 finger-widths below the kneecap, 1 finger-width outward. Boosts energy and supports digestion.

Understanding reproductive stages

STRAW+10 describes patterns across reproductive life, the menopause transition and postmenopause. It does not turn age alone into a stage.

This page explains the framework and summarizes what you enter. It does not classify your stage, ovarian age or fertility.

What clinicians look at

Bleeding patterns over time matter. The research criteria include recurring changes between consecutive cycle lengths and long gaps without bleeding, interpreted in context.

Menopause is identified retrospectively after 12 months without a period in an appropriate clinical context. Hormonal treatment can make bleeding patterns difficult to interpret.

Bleeding changes can have different explanations. Discuss pregnancy, postpartum or breastfeeding, PCOS, surgery and medicine history with your clinician. A cycle gap alone does not establish menopause.

# Protocol brief: Rhythme Learn videos (Gold Drop step 2), 20 Oct 2026

Reader and decision: Jamie (owner). Decide which short educational videos to produce for the Learn desk (the in-app computer), which existing clips to retire, and the production spec for a landscape, streamable version. Production needs his approval; this pass makes no media.

Question (PICO-style):
- Population: adult women 18+ (Singapore first, also overseas friends) using Rhythme in one of five contexts: everyday adult cycle health, trying to conceive, after pregnancy (including pregnancy loss), perimenopause, postmenopause.
- Intervention: 60 to 120 second narrated educational videos with captions, each with one purpose, plain-language key messages, a red-flag line and a see-a-doctor line.
- Comparison: the six existing square clips (17 to 28 s, old brand, no transcript track), or text-only Learn pages.
- Outcomes: accurate understanding of what is typical, when to seek care, and what the app cannot tell her; no false reassurance; no diagnosis.
- Timeframe: evidence current to October 2026.

Eligible evidence: clinical guidelines from NICE, ACOG, RCOG, ESHRE, FIGO, WHO, The Menopause Society (NAMS), IMS, MOH Singapore / HealthHub / KKH / SingHealth, Singapore Guidelines on Management of the Menopause Transition (Feb 2026); Cochrane and other systematic reviews; landmark cohort or trial papers for decisive numbers. Patient pages from NHS or HealthHub only as corroboration or for Singapore service details.
Exclusions: vendor or app marketing, influencer content, search snippets, model summaries without the opened page.

Search: PubMed via E-utilities (sequential), guideline sites opened directly (Playwright when WebFetch is refused), Codex gpt-6-astra with web search as Gate D before drafting and again on the draft.

Competing explanations and things that would change the plan:
- A newer guideline that changes a threshold (cycle length limits, when to seek fertility help, HRT framing, PCOS diagnostic criteria or name).
- Evidence that short videos mislead (oversimplification), or that an app should not present a topic without clinician context (pregnancy loss, postpartum psychosis).
- Accessibility or streaming constraints that rule out a format (caption contrast, file size on GitHub Pages, Safari range requests through the service worker).

Commercial interest: Jamie owns the app. Video topics that drive logging or engagement need extra scrutiny for overclaiming (for example "the app pinpoints your fertile window").

Standing rules: 18+ only; no diagnosis claims; urgent-care lines in every clinical video; no guaranteed outcomes; no em dashes; MiniMax never used; no media generation or TTS in this session.

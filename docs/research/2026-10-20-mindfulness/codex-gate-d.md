**Gate D judgment: retain a narrower conclusion.** Some mindfulness programmes can modestly improve selected outcomes, but the ledger does not establish which individual components work for each of the four life stages in a self-guided Singapore app. Several “permitted wordings” overstate causality, equivalence, or applicability.

This was a targeted overturn search, not a new systematic review or recalculated meta-analysis. **FT** means relevant original full-text methods/results were checked; **A** means verification was limited to the original abstract.

**1. Strongest contrary evidence by life stage and component**

| Life stage | Evidence the ledger misses or underweights |
|---|---|
| **Trying to conceive** | **Bai et al., 2019, *Human Reproduction***: 234 women undergoing their first IVF cycle received guided self-help involving breathing/body scan. Depression and sleep improved versus routine care, but **anxiety, infertility-related stress and pregnancy rates did not**. This qualifies L20’s exceptionally large pooled effects. It also tests a package with guidance, not isolated components or ordinary TTC. **A.** [Original study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31242506/) |
| **After birth and breastfeeding** | **Sun et al., 2021, *JMIR***: the antenatal depression benefit did **not** persist as a significant between-group difference at six weeks postpartum: −1.11 EPDS points, 95% CI −3.00 to 0.79, p=.25. “52.4% completed” meant at least **four of eight weeks**. **FT.** **Gammer et al., 2020, *Mindfulness***: online compassion training in 206 postpartum mothers improved self-compassion, but primary wellbeing benefit was small and sensitive to missing-data handling; other secondary outcomes did not improve. **A.** Neither establishes breastfeeding-specific efficacy. [Sun](https://www.jmir.org/2021/1/e23410/), [Gammer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12671-020-01306-9) |
| **Perimenopause** | **Wong et al., 2018, *Scientific Reports***: 197 Hong Kong women received MBSR or matched menopause education. MBSR improved total menopausal symptom scores, but **not vasomotor symptoms, perceived stress or quality of life relative to education**. The anxiety finding became nonsignificant after missing-data imputation, p=.070. This is a more informative comparator than Carmody’s waitlist. **FT.** [Original study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-24945-4) |
| **After menopause** | The Wong trial included **early postmenopause**, not the entire postmenopausal lifespan. For vasomotor symptoms, **Huang et al., 2015, *Obstetrics & Gynecology*** found breathing inferior to music, while the **2023 NAMS statement, *Menopause*** explicitly does not recommend paced respiration or mindfulness-based interventions for treating vasomotor symptoms. These findings do not prohibit mindfulness for general wellbeing. **FT passages/A.** [Huang](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4418033/), [NAMS](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37252752/) |

Two further **antenatal** null trials undermine transferring pregnancy evidence into the requested stages:

- **Hulsbosch et al., 2023, *Journal of Affective Disorders***, n=219: self-guided online mindfulness did not outperform control for pregnancy distress. **A.** [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37054897/)
- **Park et al., 2025, *JMIR***, n=133: no significant between-group effect on primary anxiety, depression or stress outcomes, despite positive abstract conclusions based partly on within-group changes; dropout was 30%. **FT.** [Study](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e56601/)

For the component question, the following limitations apply to **all four stages unless a stage-specific study is identified above**. Missing direct evidence is not evidence of ineffectiveness.

| Component | Strongest challenge to the claim |
|---|---|
| **Formal guided meditation** | **Sparacio et al., 2025, *JMIR Mental Health***: Singapore smartphone pilot, n=60, three daily ten-minute sessions, found no advantage over a structurally matched sham for reported stress or HRV. Both groups improved. Short feasibility trial, so it cannot exclude benefits from longer courses. **FT.** [Study](https://mental.jmir.org/2025/1/e77793) |
| **Breathing practice** | **Fincham et al., 2023, *Scientific Reports***: n=400, four weeks of approximately 5.5 breaths/minute versus credible, expectancy-matched 12 breaths/minute breathing. **No differential stress, anxiety, depression or wellbeing benefit.** This is a major omission from L45. It challenges the specific slow rate, not every possible breathing intervention. **A.** [Study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-49279-8) |
| **Informal everyday practice** | **Canby et al., published 2021, *Frontiers in Psychology***: informal-practice frequency did not predict improvement. Positive surveys/cohorts cannot resolve reverse causation or distinguish practice quality from generally feeling better. Neither direction establishes causality. **A.** [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33584439/) |
| **One-line micro prompts** | Schumer’s “brief” interventions ranged from inductions to two-week programmes. They do **not** establish effects of one-line prompts. Ratcliff’s ten-minute parent intervention was null against an attention control, but also does not test one-line prompts. [Schumer, 2018, *JCCP*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29939051/), [Ratcliff, 2025, *Journal of Family Psychology*; A](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40489175/) |
| **Self-compassion** | **Mak et al., 2018, *JMIR Mental Health***: mindfulness, self-compassion and CBT psychoeducation apps showed no differential wellbeing/distress improvement; only 508/2,161 completed postassessment. Gammer supplies the more directly relevant postpartum caution above. **FT.** [Mak](https://mental.jmir.org/2018/4/e60/) |
| **Body scan** | Gan’s 2022 review is weak support for durable health outcomes. However, an overly negative conclusion would also be wrong: **Sparacio et al., 2024, *Nature Human Behaviour***, n=2,239, found short-term benefit versus story listening, largest for body scan. Participants were predominantly young, and this was an immediate state outcome, not treatment of persistent symptoms. **A.** [Gan](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35538557/), [Sparacio](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01907-7) |
| **Loving-kindness** | **Petrovic et al., 2024, *Clinical Psychology Review*** found benefits versus passive controls, but no significant advantage over active controls or alternative treatments. That does not establish equivalence or stage-specific efficacy. **A.** [Review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38652973/) |
| **Just-in-time prompts** | **von Lützow et al., 2025, *BMJ Mental Health*** pooled diverse intervention packages, mostly CBT; only four studies were mindfulness-only. Wellbeing was null, g=.03, CI −.19 to .25. Its overall g=.15 does **not isolate the added value of timing or notifications**. [Original review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12481328/) |
| **Structured multi-week courses** | Positive coached/group trials cannot establish the same benefit without that support. Hulsbosch’s digital null and Sun’s postpartum null matter. The ledger’s “5–8 weeks is better” moderator is not a randomized duration comparison. |
| **Game-like rewards** | **Six et al., 2021, *JMIR Mental Health*** found no significant moderator association, not proof rewards never help. Conversely, **Moskowitz et al., 2021, *JMIR*** found virtual badges combined with facilitator contact increased skills accessed. Neither establishes clinical benefit from rewards in this PICO. **FT.** [Six](https://mental.jmir.org/2021/11/e32199/), [Badge trial](https://www.jmir.org/2021/9/e25922/) |
| **Faith-adapted practice** | **Saged et al., 2022, *Journal of Religion and Health*** included only 30 women within 62 Muslim patients, without an app or life-stage comparison. **Ahmad et al., 2026, *Harvard Review of Psychiatry*** is a scoping review and reports no standardized protocol. Neither establishes comparative efficacy, universal acceptability or safety in Singapore’s diverse faith communities. **A.** [Saged](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34981449/), [Ahmad](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42725621/) |

**Harms require counterevidence too.** **Simonsson et al., 2026, *JMIR Mental Health*** randomized 5,049 US/UK adults to a single five- or ten-minute meditation or ordinary internet use. Distressing experiences did not differ significantly, OR 1.05, CI .76–1.47; negative-affect worsening was less likely after meditation. This challenges applying intensive-programme harm rates to brief practices, while leaving repeated-use and life-stage-specific safety unresolved. **FT.** [Trial](https://mental.jmir.org/2026/1/e95628/)

**2. Alternative explanations and the most fragile claims**

- **Expectancy, attention and scheduled rest:** improvements shared by sham, breathing-control and education groups can arise without the proposed specific mechanism. This does not mean benefits are imaginary.
- **Regression to the mean and natural change:** selecting people during high distress makes uncontrolled improvement particularly uninformative. Park illustrates why improvement in one arm, but not another, is not itself a treatment difference.
- **Attrition and selection:** completers can differ in motivation, symptoms and available time. Sun, Mak and Gammer make this consequential. “Intention-to-treat” still depends on defensible missing-data assumptions.
- **Waitlist comparisons:** they do not match treatment expectation, attention or activity. They weaken component-specific interpretations of L11, L21, L44 and L46.
- **Bundled treatment:** coaching, CBT, psychoeducation, partner involvement and meditation often arrive together. Their total effect cannot be assigned to a body scan, an avatar, or acceptance instructions.
- **Publication bias:** trim-and-fill is a sensitivity model, not recovery of the known true effect.

The most fragile claims are **L20’s very large infertility effects; L17/L48’s equivalence language; L23’s attribution to timely prompts; L24/L25’s categorical reward conclusions; L29’s universal active-ingredient prescription; and L36’s “people who ruminate gain most.”** Predictor findings and study-level moderators are not validated treatment-selection rules.

L30’s placebo explanation also remains a hypothesis: its full text explicitly leaves the contribution of therapeutic components versus digital placebo unresolved. [Linardon, 2020, *Behavior Therapy*, FT](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Linardon2020.pdf)

**3. Population and context mismatches**

The largest mismatch is **pregnancy versus the actual PICO**: pregnancy is neither TTC nor postpartum breastfeeding. Antenatal benefits cannot fill those cells.

Other important transfers are:

- **Infertility/IVF patients → routine TTC:** different distress, treatment exposure and outcome expectations.
- **Students → reproductive and menopausal populations:** Schumer included 46 student studies out of 65; mean age was approximately 25.
- **Clinical recurrent depression → general wellness:** MBCT relapse prevention concerns selected patients, structured treatment and often concurrent care.
- **Teacher-led groups → self-guided apps:** social support, screening and responses to distress change.
- **Early natural or treatment-induced menopause → all postmenopause:** these are not interchangeable.
- **China, Iran, Hong Kong, US/UK → Singapore:** language, faith fit, clinical pathways and digital trust require testing. Geography alone does not invalidate evidence, but it cannot establish local acceptability.

Even the Singapore sham trial excluded people with mental-illness histories and required English proficiency and age ≥21. It therefore does not cover the full requested population. [Singapore trial](https://mental.jmir.org/2025/1/e77793)

**4. Remove the weakest evidence: does the conclusion flip?**

A statistical leave-one-out analysis cannot honestly be performed from this ledger alone. The following is a **qualitative dependency check**, not a reanalysis; “smallest” is not synonymous with “weakest.”

| Cluster | Removal check and consequence |
|---|---|
| General app benefits/formal courses | Removing one small positive trial would not erase the broad pooled signal. It would still not identify effective components or establish this PICO. |
| TTC | Remove Galhardo’s nonrandomized study: some depression/sleep support remains from Bai, but broad anxiety, stress and fertility claims remain unsupported. |
| Postpartum | Remove Sun from postpartum support, because its postpartum endpoint is null: confidence falls, and postpartum-specific trials must carry the conclusion. |
| Self-compassion/body image | Remove Papini’s pilot: the ledger loses its direct pregnancy/postpartum body-shame evidence. Broader self-compassion findings do not replace that endpoint. |
| Menopause bother | Remove Carmody’s waitlist study: the specific bother narrative weakens materially. Active-control evidence still does not establish a vasomotor treatment. |
| Breathing | Remove Huang: Carpenter’s null remains. Remove either small positive stress trial: the n=400 matched-control null still requires qualification. |
| Sleep | The updated digital review remained positive after excluding five high-risk studies, but evidence certainty remained very low and active-treatment superiority was unestablished. [Lee & Park, 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-02120-0) |
| Informal practice/micro prompts | Removing a positive association leaves no demonstrated causal effect. Removing a null study does not create positive evidence. |
| Body scan/loving-kindness | Immediate state benefits remain plausible; durable clinical and stage-specific claims remain uncertain. |
| JIT prompts | The review’s outlier removal barely changed g=.15 to .16. This supports robustness of the **package estimate**, not timing as the mechanism. |
| Rewards | Remove Deci’s laboratory evidence: no basis remains for claiming virtual rewards necessarily undermine adult app motivation. Six still does not establish universal ineffectiveness. |
| Faith adaptation | Remove Saged: direct clinical support becomes substantially thinner; cultural adaptation remains a design proposition, not demonstrated treatment efficacy. |
| Harms | Remove Britton: adverse experiences remain documented, but its exact lasting-effect percentage loses support. No universal app-risk estimate survives. |

**6. Important missing evidence**

- **PMDD:** Panahi’s PMS trial excluded PMDD. **Bluth et al., 2015, *Mindfulness*** used historical controls for menstrual-related mood disorders; one participant withdrew with heightened anxiety. Neither establishes mindfulness-app treatment for PMDD. [Panahi](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5153465/), [Bluth, FT](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4651211/)
- **Menopause-specific digital CBT:** **Stute et al., 2026, *Maturitas*** tested a self-guided app in 100 women. Hot-flush problem rating improved versus usual care by 1.47 points, CI .65–2.29. It is an open-label, sponsor-associated pilot, but a directly relevant alternative omitted from the ledger. **A.** [Trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42641440/)
- **Digital sleep evidence:** **Lee and Park, 2025, *npj Digital Medicine*** found sleep g=.38, falling to .19 under trim-and-fill; certainty was **very low**, and active-control comparisons were inconclusive. **Garland et al., 2014, *Journal of Clinical Oncology*** found MBSR failed noninferiority to CBT-I immediately after treatment, although it met the criterion at follow-up. [Review, FT](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-02120-0), [Garland, A](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2012.47.7265)
- **Direct digital harms:** **Beloborodova et al., 2026, *JMIR Mental Health*** reported adverse experiences and functional impairment in digital meditation studies. Its meditation versus didactic-only comparison was not randomized, so it cannot determine attributable risk. [Study, FT](https://mental.jmir.org/2026/1/e90602)
- **Notification burden and reward pressure:** a 2025 *Proceedings of the Design Society* qualitative study found reminders/streaks could motivate some users and feel intrusive or guilt-inducing to others. This supports monitoring user experience, not a quantified clinical-harm claim. [Study, FT](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/proceedings-of-the-design-society/article/mindfulness-and-the-unseen-understanding-the-impact-of-dark-patterns-in-mindfulness-applications/C238118AB4D1185F1967826A0267348A)
- **Women’s-health privacy:** **Malki et al., CHI 2024** documented inconsistent policies/features, flawed consent/deletion mechanisms and sensitive-data collection. These are separate harms that meditation efficacy trials do not capture. [Original research record](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10188933/)
- **Still unverified:** breastfeeding-specific effects, Singapore life-stage subgroups, long-term app engagement, component-specific harms, and whether faith adaptation improves outcomes beyond a culturally respectful secular option. I did not verify enough full-text evidence to resolve these gaps or audit every constituent trial in L14/L20.

**Claims I would not let through**

- **“These components work across all four life stages.”** The required component-by-stage evidence is absent.
- **“Sun demonstrates postpartum efficacy.”** Its postpartum endpoint was null.
- **“Mindfulness improves conception chances.”** No established fertility benefit.
- **“Mindfulness or breathing treats hot flushes.”** Contrary active-control and guideline evidence.
- **“Mindfulness sleep apps are as effective as CBT-I.”** Nonsignificance is not equivalence.
- **“One-minute prompts are proven helpful, harmless, or pleasant.”** The cited interventions do not establish those claims.
- **“Acceptance is the active ingredient, so every practice must include it.”** Specific dismantling results do not establish universal necessity.
- **“Rewards do not work” or “rewards undermine motivation.”** Both exceed the app-specific causal evidence.
- **“Timely prompts help a little.”** The pooled estimate concerns intervention packages, not timing alone.
- **“A quarter to half stop using the apps.”** Torous measured trial dropout/postassessment completion, not directly observed app abandonment. [Original paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165032719326060)
- **“8.3% experience harm” or “6–14% lasting harm” as app-user forecasts.** Wrong exposure, population, denominator and causal interpretation.
- **“The corrected effect is .10/.04” or “benefits are digital placebo.”** Sensitivity analyses and plausible explanations are being presented as settled facts.

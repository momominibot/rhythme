# Daily guidance: evidence and release scope

Source check: 1 October 2026. Local educational implementation, not validated clinical triage or an ovarian-health prediction model. Clinical release review remains pending.

## Decision and evidence protocol

Help someone choose a small useful action today from explicitly entered health context and dated records. Eligible evidence: first-party clinical guidance and a full professional position statement. Exclude vendor cycle-sync claims, phase-specific performance prescriptions, symptom-derived ovarian-age scores and automatic treatment changes. An independent reviewer searched the question before seeing implementation; the root checked the underlying sources. No clinical endorsement is implied.

The selected appearance and teaching-example ring are never health inputs. Context is separately confirmed. Thirty-day reconfirmation and seven-day observation windows are software freshness rules, not clinical thresholds. Current check-ins are selected by local calendar day. Older records remain intact; omitted records or unanswered questions do not establish normal health. Historical care notes must retain their recorded date.

## Implemented routing versus clinical approval

The final code and test receipt specify which of these narrow rules are implemented. The ledger does not imply all clinical fields or alarm rules exist. The app does not read arbitrary notes as a diagnosis, give a comprehensive all-clear, prescribe fasting, change contraception or use TCM labels as measured biomedical findings. The persistent help text directs serious symptoms to appropriate local care independently of what is logged.

Clinical review must examine field choices, missingness, context conflicts, date windows and exact wording before public release. Device-only health records have no account isolation or application-layer encryption; new web sign-in does not silently upload or protect them.

# Specialist feedback: cycle observations and clinical context

Reviewed 11 September 2026. Research and implementation recommendations; not clinician approval or a validated prediction model. No clinician contacted or clinical records collected. A bounded correction to the legacy cycle-log preview was applied after the source review; structured measurement logging remains a separate task.

## Decision

Build an optional, consistent observation log with a dated treatment/lab/device timeline first. Keep observations, external estimates and clinician interpretations visibly separate. Do not introduce an ovarian-age score, automatic ovulation confirmation, treatment changes or a discharge-photo accuracy claim from this evidence.

The specialist's concern is supported: useful, affordable observations remain imperfect proxies. The appropriate response is better measurement instructions and provenance, not invented numerical confidence. Photos are a research candidate, not an established upgrade.

## Findings and limits

| Question | Directly supported finding | Ovuluna decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is cervical mucus too subjective to use? | ACOG describes changes in appearance **and sensation** and lists medicines, hygiene products, douching, intercourse, breastfeeding and lubricant used during pelvic examination as confounders. CDC independently identifies discharge, bleeding and some medicines as factors complicating fertility-sign interpretation. [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/fertility-awareness-based-methods-of-family-planning), [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/contraception/hcp/usmec/fertility-awareness-based-methods.html) | Offer optional standardized appearance/sensation observations, an “unsure” choice and contextual flags. Avoid judging hygiene or treating a color selection as a diagnosis. |
| Can self-observation still help? | A secondary analysis of a randomized trial included 57 instructed women and 187 complete cycles. Women identified a mucus Peak Day in only 117 cycles; among those, agreement with **LH surge + 1 day** was 25% on the exact day, 58% within ±1 day and 84% within ±2 days. This reference is itself an ovulation estimate. It supports useful tracking with instruction, not exact-day certainty. [Stanford et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32101336/) ASRM also supports mucus monitoring when optimizing natural fertility. [ASRM natural fertility](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/optimizing-natural-fertility-a-committee-opinion-2021/) | Preserve missing/uninterpretable days. Do not equate “dry” with a safe day, or one observation with ovulation or an estrogen measurement. |
| Do photos improve accuracy? | A 2024 InVivo study used a pictorial dictionary, daily photographs and substantial instructor support: 32 women, 108 cycle charts; a 10-woman subset supplied 32 macro/microscopic image pairs. It did not isolate an automated smartphone-photo classifier against manual observation in an independent ovulation-accuracy trial. The method's creator was an investigator. [Stachowska et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11172004/) | There is preliminary work worth considering, but no verified basis here to claim an Ovuluna photo is more accurate. First use reviewed teaching illustrations. Any future image feature needs its own prospectively tested intended use and explicit intimate-image privacy controls. |
| Is smartphone image research transferable? | A separate smartphone study analyzed **air-dried saliva**, using an optical attachment and microfluidic device; its human testing involved six women and urine-test comparison. It did not classify ordinary photographs of vaginal discharge. [Smartphone saliva study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6321627/) | Do not borrow saliva-ferning, microscopy or cervical-cancer image performance numbers for discharge photos. A static photograph also omits sensation and the preceding daily pattern; this is a measurement-design limitation, not a quantified accuracy estimate. |
| What should BBT do? | ACOG recommends a reading after waking and before activity, food or drink, and notes fever can interfere. ASRM calls daily BBT inexpensive but often unreliable; NICE NG257 explicitly advises against BBT charts to confirm ovulation during infertility assessment. [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/fertility-awareness-based-methods-of-family-planning), [ASRM evaluation](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/fertility-evaluation-of-infertile-women-a-committee-opinion-2021/), [NICE 1.18.9](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng257/chapter/Investigation-of-fertility-problems-and-management-strategies) | Offer optional daily temperature recording with consistent method and timing. Display the raw trend and affected/missing readings; do not label a single rise “ovulation confirmed.” Extra adherence alone does not prove accuracy. |
| What about urine LH brands and follow-up? | FDA says correct cycle timing, procedure and brand-specific reading instructions matter. ASRM says urine LH is indirect evidence; products vary and false positive/negative results occur. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/home-use-tests/ovulation-urine-test), [ASRM evaluation](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/fertility-evaluation-of-infertile-women-a-committee-opinion-2021/) | Save brand/model, specimen, test time, result format and invalid/missed results. Follow the actual kit's instructions; do not apply one universal clock-time rule or convert a visual line ratio into a lab concentration. |
| Should blood tests and specialist treatment be included? | ASRM requires reserve results to be interpreted with age, risk factors and previous treatment/response. Appropriately timed serum progesterone can support recent ovulation, but a single value does not establish luteal quality. [ASRM evaluation](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/fertility-evaluation-of-infertile-women-a-committee-opinion-2021/) AMH/AFC principally inform follicle quantity and treatment response, not egg quality or a standalone fertility forecast; hormonal contraception can affect AMH. [ASRM reserve](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/testing-and-interpreting-measures-of-ovarian-reserve-a-committee-opinion-2020/) ACOG also advises against using one AMH result to predict time to pregnancy in presumed-fertile women. [ACOG AMH](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2019/04/the-use-of-antimullerian-hormone-in-women-not-seeking-fertility-care) | Add a treatment-context timeline and retain exact reports, units, collection date, assay and clinician notes. Record prescribed hormones/trigger injections and their timing. Keep clinical interpretation with the treating team; do not issue automatic treatment, fasting or supplement changes. |
| Can FemTech readings be combined? | Apple's own Cycle Tracking estimates are retrospective and do not guarantee ovulation. FDA's Natural Cycles clearance evaluated a specific algorithm/input combination; it is not validation of another app using the same watch. [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-euro/120357), [FDA K231274](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/K231274.pdf) | Distinguish waking oral temperature, sleeping wrist temperature and an external app's derived ovulation estimate. Save device/app/version and import history. Avoid averaging these into one interchangeable signal or copying an outside app's confidence. |

**Guideline difference to preserve:** NICE NG257 (published 31 March 2026; recommendations 1.18.7–1.18.9 retain earlier evidence) recommends mid-luteal progesterone in infertility investigations even with regular cycles. ASRM does not require additional ovulation testing when menstrual history is clearly adequate, with relevant clinical exceptions. Both support context-sensitive assessment and neither validates an Ovuluna BBT diagnosis. Therefore the app should record a clinician's test plan, not automatically order tests or fix all progesterone collection to cycle day 21. [NICE](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng257/chapter/Investigation-of-fertility-problems-and-management-strategies), [ASRM](https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/fertility-evaluation-of-infertile-women-a-committee-opinion-2021/)

## Actual source review and priorities

Verified in the active continuation, not inferred from an earlier app:

1. **P0 corrected — legacy cycle-log claims and false save affordance.** `cycle-log.html` now uses descriptive appearance/texture labels without infection diagnoses, hormonal inferences, pregnancy reassurance or fertility-dot ratings. Fixed personal dates, preselected observations and the unreviewed cold-drink prescription were removed. The page explicitly identifies itself as an unsaved observation preview and links to the actual `wellness-checkin.html` journal, where a user can enter and save a dated note. No preview selections are transferred. The misleading Save/cloud-sync claim and unsaved free-text box were removed. Reviewed ACOG/CDC context and NHS care links accompany the descriptions. The existing artwork/card composition is retained; small-screen bubble, image and bleeding-swatch grids reflow, and preview controls support keyboard selection. `appnav.js` was read: its generic behavior adds ripples and routes dead `#` links; no save interception was found. The new journal link has a real destination.
2. **P1 — add a basic observation contract and clinical-context entry.** `ovarian-model.js` currently stores age/cycle/context plus optional dated AMH and keeps all numerical predictions null. `labs-records.js` already retains analyte, qualifier, original units, collection date, source, assay and free-text context. `home-records.js` stores six dated check-in categories but no BBT, mucus or urine-test observations. Build on these contracts instead of inventing a parallel score.
3. **P1 — make records portable and deletable.** Add every new store to the reviewed Settings inventory, export and deletion flow. A clinician review sheet should contain original observations and gaps. Generating an export is separate from sending it; sharing requires the user's action.
4. **P2 — validate interpretation separately.** Only after a clinician has specified population, target outcome, reference method, exclusions and acceptable error should a versioned estimator be studied. Photo interpretation and paid wearable integration come after basic logging and privacy, not before them.

These priorities and the following fields are **product design choices informed by the evidence**, not a published scoring formula.

## Proposed record fields — not implemented by this review

Use one versioned observation envelope with a discriminated `kind`; keep the existing lab schema compatible through an explicit migration rather than overwriting old entries.

| Record part | Minimum fields and behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Common | `id`, `schemaVersion`, `kind`, `observedOn`, optional actual local time/time-zone offset, `recordedAt`, `updatedAt`, `sourceType` (`self-observed`, `user-transcribed`, `device-import`, `clinician-entered`), `sourceRecordId` for imports, `missingReason`, `contextFlags`. A typed claim is still user-transcribed until a real verification process exists. |
| Bleeding/cycle | Actual first full-flow date separate from spotting; cycle ID derived only from saved dates; unknown onset remains unknown. |
| BBT | Original finite value/unit, measurement site/method, thermometer/device, waking measurement time, before-activity status and optional disrupted-sleep/fever flags. Wrist readings receive a different kind. No zero defaults or imputed measurements. |
| Mucus | Observation method/time, separate appearance and sensation, optional stretch observation, unsure/not-observed, relevant products/intercourse/bleeding/symptoms. No fertility score attached to a selection. |
| Urine tests | Analyte exactly identified, brand/model, collected/tested/read times when known, qualitative result or original numeric result with its stated unit, invalid/uncertain, instructions followed/unknown, optional expiry/lot. Preserve strip-reader output separately from a manual reading and from serum LH. |
| Labs | Existing exact result/qualifier/unit/date/source/assay; add specimen, known cycle day plus its provenance, treatment-context link and optional report identifier. Blood progesterone and urinary PdG must remain explicitly different test kinds. |
| Treatment | Optional treatment type, medicine name as entered, start/stop or administration dates, linked clinician plan and pregnancy/postpartum/breastfeeding/hormonal-contraception context. “Prefer not to say” remains possible; omitted context must not silently mean untreated. |
| Device import | Manufacturer/model, source app/version, original sample identifier, measurement interval/time zone, raw-versus-derived status, import time and permission state. Preserve upstream corrections/deletions and deduplicate repeat imports. |
| Interpretation | Separate derived record with method version, referenced input IDs, supported population, result type and limitations. Current ovarian-age/ovulation predictions remain unavailable; a completion percentage may describe answered fields only, never biological confidence. |

Do not collect intimate photographs in this first implementation. A future research protocol would need explicit opt-in, standardized capture instructions, secure storage/access/deletion, no automatic third-party AI upload, and independent evaluation across devices and relevant clinical contexts. Compare photo-assisted and observation-only approaches on participant-separated data; count rejected/missing images and false reassurance, not only accuracy among easy images. These are proposed acceptance criteria, not evidence that such validation has occurred.

## Verification scope

Implementation check: `node scripts/check-site.cjs --output /private/tmp/ovuluna-specialist-site-static.json` parsed the changed page without errors. The first check found an unrelated Settings link to missing `wellness.html`, corrected by its owning agent. Final check: 76 pages, 230 parsed scripts, 825 local page links; no syntax/missing-target failures. Two computed markup references remain unresolved by static analysis. This is not proof of all-page runtime or clinical correctness.

An isolated routed Chromium origin exercised descriptive results, dry-text non-reassurance, repeat-click deselection, Enter-key selection, no health-record writes, absence of fake Save/free-text traps, actual journal navigation without a success toast, and 320px/A++ light/dark reflow. Receipt: `/private/tmp/ovuluna-specialist-ui/receipt.json`; screenshots beside it. Fonts were routed to the native build's pinned local font assets. Initial checks identified a test preference-key mismatch and genuine fixed-grid/swatch overflow; the final source passed after those were corrected. Visual inspection also found a tall-card backdrop-filter paint defect in light mode; disabling backdrop blur only on this page fixed it. The final light/dark screenshots use unmodified application source after that correction. This check used no live backend, native device or real user data.

Care wording was checked against [NHS discharge advice](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/vaginal-discharge/), [NHS pelvic-pain escalation](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/pelvic-pain/) and [CDC vaginitis assessment](https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/vaginal-discharge.htm). It routes symptom concerns to care rather than assigning a disease to a color.


This was a focused primary-source search and source-code review, not a systematic review. PubMed abstracts and retrievable primary article sections were read; some ACOG, NICE and PMC direct opens returned access errors, so their indexed first-party text was checked against independent guidance where available. No claim of exhaustive absence of photo research is made. The absence of a verified model for this app is the decisive limitation.

The small image and mucus studies do not establish clinical accuracy for Ovuluna, and their populations/comparators should not be generalized to all treatment, postpartum or irregular-cycle contexts. No arithmetic weights, menopause date, individual ovarian age or TCM diagnosis were created. This report does not close clinical review, protected storage, authentication, account deletion or device-validation release gates.

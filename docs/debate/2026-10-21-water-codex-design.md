The design needs revision before building. The main blockers are volume-based rewards, an unsupported personalised formula, incomplete safety screening, and storage behaviour that can lose or resurrect records. No files were changed.

**1. Evidence.** Most quoted figures are real, but several are applied beyond their source’s scope.

| Source or figure | Review |
|---|---|
| **EFSA 2010: 2.0 L/day for women** | Correct for **total water**, including food and all beverages, under moderate temperature and activity. Pregnancy **+300 ml** and lactation **+700 ml** are also correct total-water additions. They are not instructions to add those amounts to a separately derived plain-water target. The publisher’s abstract was accessible during this review. [EFSA](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/1459) |
| **ESPEN 2022 R61: 1.6 L drinks for women, 2.0 L for men** | Correct, Grade B, with the clinical-condition exception. It specifically concerns **older people** and says to offer at least those amounts. Its commentary derives 1.6 L using an assumed 80% beverage contribution to EFSA’s total. The 20–30% food contribution is an approximation, not a measured allowance for this user. [ESPEN, page 4](https://congress.espen.org/files/ESPEN-Guidelines/ESPEN_practical_guideline_Clinical_nutrition_and_hydration_in_geriatrics.pdf) |
| **HPB: eight glasses, approximately two litres** | Correctly described as general guidance **including fluid from food**. The article also mentions greater needs with sweating, exercise, pregnancy, breastfeeding and illness. I could not verify the claimed **27 June 2025 review date** from the retrieved page. [HealthHub: The Best Refreshment](https://www.healthhub.sg/well-being-and-lifestyle/food-diet-and-nutrition/the-best-refreshment) |
| **NHS: 6–8 cups/glasses** | Correct for **fluids**, including tea, coffee and milk. The page supports pale-yellow urine and contextual increases, but supplies neither a fixed glass size nor a personalised kilogram formula. [NHS hydration guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/food-guidelines-and-food-labels/water-drinks-nutrition/) |
| **NAM/IOM 2005: 2.7 L total, approximately 80% from fluids** | Correct. The report describes approximately **2.2 L from beverages**, about 81%, for women aged 19–30. It establishes no tolerable upper intake level and discusses acute toxicity after intake greatly exceeds approximately **0.7–1.0 L/hour** excretion capacity. That capacity is not a safe drinking-rate recommendation. [Institute of Medicine, 2005](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10925/chapter/2) |
| **CKD WIT 2018** | The effect estimate is correct: **−0.3 ml/min/1.73 m²**, 95% CI **−1.8 to 1.2**, **P=.74**. Say that increased-water coaching did not significantly slow decline over one year in **stage 3 CKD**, with acknowledged power limitations. “More water does not help the kidneys in CKD” is too broad. [Original JAMA trial](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2680548) |
| **Hew-Butler 2017** | Supports avoiding overdrinking and thirst-guided intake around exercise. It does **not** validate a universal daily 4 L ceiling, a 4.5 L stone threshold, or thirst-only advice for every clinical population. [Original review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2017.00021/full) |

The per-kilogram discussion needs correction. **30–35 ml/kg does appear in NICE CG32**, for clinician-managed nutrition support, accounting for food, drinks and other fluid sources. Calling it absent from guidelines is incorrect. Applying it to a healthy-user drinking calculator is a separate, unsupported extrapolation. Also, 80% of 30–35 is **24–28**, not a uniquely justified 25. [NICE CG32, 1.4.1–1.4.2](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg32/chapter/Recommendations)

**My recommendation is no weight adjustment in version one.** Labelling the formula “Rhythme’s rule of thumb” is transparent, but does not establish its suitability. Neither the 25 ml/kg choice nor its 1.5–2.5 L clamp is validated by these sources. The lower clamp also falls below the older-women recommendation being used to justify it.

“Within the EFSA and HPB range” does not solve this: their quoted figures concern total water, while the proposed strip records plain water. Keep the plain-water record separate from general drinking information, without calculating an amount she personally “requires”.

**2. Safety.** The three modes should become **general information plus logging**, and **logging without numerical advice**. Kidney stones should be a care-team note, not a separate reward mode.

The screening needs these changes:

- **Explicit low blood sodium and SIADH.** “Low sodium” can mean a low-salt diet. Do not bury a diagnosed condition inside the fluid-restriction question. [NHS England SIADH policy](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/clin-comm-pol-16051P.pdf)
- **Cirrhosis with ascites.** Route to individual advice without an app number. Do not imply everyone with ascites needs restriction; current guidance says restriction is not routine for most patients. [KASL 2026 guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13430399/)
- **Medicines affecting fluid management, particularly lithium.** Lithium needs adequate fluid intake and attention to significant fluid/sodium changes. “Care” must not become shorthand for drinking less. [NHS Specialist Pharmacy Service](https://sps.nhs.uk/monitorings/lithium-monitoring/)
- **Pre-eclampsia and clinician-managed pregnancy complications.** These override generic pregnancy additions. Hospital maintenance-fluid recommendations must not become home drinking targets. [NICE NG133](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng133/chapter/Recommendations)
- **Eating disorders and water loading.** Offer a way to hide numbers or decline water tracking without requiring disclosure of a diagnosis. NICE specifically identifies water loading as a reason to assess fluid and electrolyte balance. [NICE NG69, 1.10.1](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng69/chapter/Recommendations)
- **Older age.** Reduced thirst can increase dehydration risk, and ESPEN R68 advises against using urine colour to assess hydration status in older adults. Age alone is not a reason to restrict fluid. The current general sentence needs that qualification. [ESPEN, page 21](https://congress.espen.org/files/ESPEN-Guidelines/ESPEN_practical_guideline_Clinical_nutrition_and_hydration_in_geriatrics.pdf)

For Singapore heat, retain contextual information without adding a fixed climate multiplier. Heat and exertion change losses, but neither supplies a universal extra allowance or overrides prescribed fluid advice.

Store **“not sure” as not sure**, not as a positive diagnosis. Unanswered, declined or incomplete screening should permit voluntary logging while withholding personalised numbers.

Question four cannot remain locked to the intro. `room.js:2640` asks about breastfeeding only when someone reports giving birth within the past year. That misses breastfeeding beyond one year and pregnancy while breastfeeding. Current breastfeeding must be independently editable. Possible pregnancy, confirmed pregnancy, postpartum and breastfeeding need distinct handling; postpartum alone must not trigger a lactation addition.

The **NHS 2.5–3 L stone-prevention quotation is correct**, and NICE agrees. However, that does not justify paying at 2.5 L or raising the warning threshold to 4.5 L. Keep the recommendation in linked information, with individual advice taking precedence. [NHS kidney stones](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/kidney-stones/), [NICE NG118](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng118/chapter/recommendations)

Remove the **1.5 L heart-failure line from care-mode UI**. The HealthHub quotation is accurate, but its contributor is **NHG Health**, not HPB, and it includes tea, coffee, soup, porridge and medication water. A plain-water log cannot monitor that total-fluid restriction. Showing a number also contradicts the proposed no-number mode. [HealthHub heart-failure guidance](https://www.healthhub.sg/health-conditions/heart-failure-monitoring-fluid-and-salt-intake)

**Yes, rewarding guide attainment can create an unsafe incentive.** A once-daily reward still encourages reaching the threshold despite discomfort, other unrecorded fluids or changed circumstances. That is a foreseeable design risk, not evidence that Rhythme has already caused harm.

Remove the **4/4.5 L “ceiling”**. Neither is an established safety boundary. “That is a lot” judges the record; “if unwell, get advice” is too vague for serious neurological symptoms. Keep neutral overdrinking information available below any threshold, with urgent symptoms routed to the existing care card. Do not infer drinking speed from logging speed: someone may enter several earlier drinks together.

**3. Product rules.** Reward the first genuine water record, independently of volume.

`ovu-rewards.js:40–44` explicitly describes one daily award per kind, no health-outcome rewards and no extra earning for amount. The economy review excludes health outcomes, and the four-persona review removed streak and meal-volume pressure.

Recommended rules:

- Six hearts for the first eligible water record of the actual day.
- The same logging award in care mode, because it rewards recording rather than consuming a prescribed amount.
- No extra hearts for more water, reaching guidance, changing eligibility, editing, Undo/re-add, backdating or importing.
- No hearts for the check-in tick or a skip answer.
- Keep food’s existing independent award and no streak.

Withholding ordinary logging hearts only from care-mode users would make disclosure financially disadvantageous within the game. If water logging cannot be rewarded neutrally, award nothing for water in any mode.

The Water tile should mean **recorded or explicitly answered**, regardless of volume. Existing check-ins count “not sure” and “nothing to log” as answered. Therefore “an answer, not a tick” conflicts with the current contract unless it means “not proof of adequate hydration”.

Use **“Not recording water today”**. It may complete that check-in without creating a zero-intake observation.

Replace Food throughout `CHECK_TILES`, `defaultKeys`, `todayCheckIn`, `renderCheckIn`, `ciOpen` and quick answers. Open `openDiary('today')`, not the default Add a meal pane. Preserve an existing opt-out when introducing the replacement tile, but **never convert historical food skip answers into water answers**.

**4. Data.** Keeping water outside diary event types is reasonable, but the proposed storage contract is incomplete.

**Concurrency is a reproduced defect.** `ovu-store.js:91–109` merges state only at its top-level keys. I ran the real store in two in-memory contexts:

- A prepared 250 ml and B prepared 200 ml. After both commits, only B’s 200 ml remained.
- After A called `clearAll()`, B’s stale water patch restored the deleted water into the empty diary.

Adding IDs inside the same nested object is insufficient without changing its merge behaviour. A suitable logical shape is:

```js
water: {
  version: 1,
  settings: { /* answers, display preferences */ },
  records: {
    "<id>": { id, date, ml, recordedAt }
  },
  tombstones: {
    "<deleted-id>": { deletedAt }
  }
}
```

Derive daily totals. Keep the observation date separate from creation time; retain an optional consumption time only if explicitly supplied.

This requires an **explicit ID-aware merge and serialized read-modify-write operations**, checking the diary owner and a reset generation before saving. Serialization must cover all writers of the shared database, including rewards and deletion. A lock used only by water cannot protect it from another module overwriting the same storage key.

**Water and rewards must save together.** `commit()` requires fresh nested objects. Current `award()` writes its receipt and reward changes separately through `setState()`, so `commit(water); award(...)` does not provide the promised rollback. Prepare copied water and reward state, then commit both once. Follow the existing `thank()`/`spend()` pattern rather than manufacturing a diary event.

**Export works, normal restore does not.** `exportJSON()` includes state, and `clearAll()` replaces it. However, `journal.html:361` calls merge import, which currently imports events and profile but ignores state. Water would be exported and then silently omitted by the normal Restore UI. Specify validated, idempotent water restore, with no restored hearts and reconfirmation of outdated eligibility.

**The AI promise needs narrower wording.** Leaving `S.TYPES` unchanged preserves Luna’s output filter. But users can write about drinking water in a note, and existing food-note paths can carry that text into summaries. Promise that **the strip’s structured records and screening answers are not automatically included** in AI, cloud, PDF or share payloads.

Keep screening answers in local state. Putting them into `profile.conditions` would expose them to existing summary context; other new profile fields can enter cloud backup through `profileForCloud()`.

**Privacy inventory already covers the database key.** Update its label and `privacy.html:41` to name water records and screening answers, and explicitly say they remain local even when diary backup is enabled.

**There is another water logger.** `hydration.html` still writes `ov_hydro`, uses 35 ml/kg and displays goals, percentages and cycle-temperature claims; `appnav.js:81` still routes there. Define its retirement or reconciliation while preserving existing records. “Nothing elsewhere counts water” is currently false.

E95–E100 are unused, but E17 already cites EFSA. Preserve permanent IDs and the E81–E90 reservation, add the `water` topic, and give separate documents separately traceable citations instead of bundling several behind one URL.

**5. Copy.** “Guide” and “about” do not neutralise a number that unlocks hearts. “About 1.6 L today” beside a logged total still reads as a target.

Prefer:

- “Water recorded today: 750 ml”
- “Add water you already drank”
- “250 ml”, “200 ml”, “500 ml”, “Other amount”
- “General drinking information”
- “Not recording water today”
- “Saved on this device. Water records are not included in cloud backup yet.”

For care mode:

> Follow your care team’s advice about fluids. This log records plain water only, so it does not show your total fluid intake.

Keep container names as examples, since glasses and cups vary. Avoid “remaining”, “on track”, “complete”, achievement animations and invitations to catch up before midnight. The decision to omit skin, weight, energy and cycle-benefit claims is appropriate.

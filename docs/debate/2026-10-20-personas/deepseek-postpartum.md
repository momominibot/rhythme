## 2. Safety red flags and exact escalation wording

At Nadia’s life stage the app must escalate:

> If you have thoughts of harming yourself or your baby, call 995 now or go to the nearest Emergency Department. Do not wait.

> To talk to someone now, call Samaritans of Singapore at 1767 or Mindline at 1771.

> If you have felt low, flat, hopeless, guilty or not yourself for more than two weeks, please see a doctor. You can walk in to any polyclinic, or contact KKH or NUH for an appointment.

> If you cannot stop crying, cannot sleep even when the baby sleeps, or feel your mind racing beyond your control, tell a doctor today.

This aligns with NICE guidance that all women should be asked about mental health at every postnatal contact and urgent referral should follow any thought of self-harm or harm to the baby (NICE CG192, https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg192).

## 4. Clinically wrong, culturally off, or likely to make Nadia stop

- **Cycle and phase advice**: The app repeatedly uses phase-based food, tea, herb and record prompts. For a breastfeeding woman whose periods have not returned, this can feel irrelevant or misleading. Lactational amenorrhea is common at 4 months postpartum, but absence of periods does not mean the app should impose a regular cycle model. This is clinically wrong for her current state. Unverified for Nadia if no postpartum mode exists.

- **Herbal and supplement suggestions**: Breastfeeding safety for herbs and teas is not automatically assured. Some herbs can affect milk supply or infant health. This must be checked against LactMed (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501922/) before display. Unverified if the app does not filter by breastfeeding safety.

- **Weight and food logging**: A weight and food system with phase-based suggestions can encourage restrictive eating. WHO recommends exclusive breastfeeding for about six months and continued breastfeeding with complementary foods thereafter, which requires adequate maternal energy and fluid intake (WHO, https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/infant-and-young-child-feeding). The app should avoid weight goals or calorie pressure.

- **Face and finger camera pulse or stress scans**: These are not medical devices and should be labelled clearly as experimental. For a mother at risk of low mood, a “stress scan” may increase health anxiety rather than help.

- **No tudung representation**: The wardrobe asset names include kebaya, pajamas, party, sundress, yoga and cozy, but no visible tudung option. For Nadia, seeing her avatar without a tudung during meditation or everyday actions may feel alien and culturally off.

- **Social and community features**: A very tired mother may find friend requests, sending notes and plaza presence another task. These should be optional, quiet and non-guilt-inducing. Fixed social phrases should not imply she must respond or participate.

- **Unvalidated acupressure and movement guidance**: Acupressure for postpartum recovery is not established. Gentle movement after birth is usually encouraged, but Nadia should be told to stop if something hurts and to seek help if leaking, pain or pressure occurs. This advice should be signposted to local care, not treated as product guidance.

# Rhythm check: how Rhythme reads symptoms, circadian rhythm and lifestyle for four life stages

Design version 2, 18 October 2026, after three independent reviews (DeepSeek, Doubao, Codex; docs/debate/2026-10-18-score-review-*.md and the reconciliation note). Status: proposal for Jamie and Dr Patrick. Nothing here is implemented; no app code was changed for this document. Method: Gold Drop lightweight mode (established, contested, gap, our angle; certainty labelled High, Moderate, Low or Very Low), a jev comb over the evidence register and the reviewers' remarks (docs/debate/2026-10-18-score-jev-comb.json), four Codex persona walkthroughs of the app as it is today, primary sources opened for every load-bearing claim (section 12), and a review round whose verdicts were "ship with the blockers fixed" (DeepSeek), "do not ship as designed" (Doubao) and "do not ship" (Codex). Version 2 adopts their corrections except where the primary source says otherwise (section 15). Where a number is a product choice rather than an evidence threshold, it says so and the user interface must say so too.

## 1. The decision in one paragraph

Rhythme should not show a wellness number. It should show a **rhythm check**: a short, deterministic read of three panels that the phone computes from what the person logged in the last 7 and 14 days: **Rhythm** (sleep timing and regularity), **Body** (symptoms, pain, mood, bleeding) and **Day** (movement, meals, caffeine, alcohol, calm minutes), plus a **Cycle** panel only when periods are expected. Each panel shows how many days were logged, what the rules noticed against a named threshold, the chip behind that threshold (an E number for an evidence threshold, a "Rhythme threshold" chip for a product choice), and one of three levels in plain words: **Nothing flagged in what you logged**, **A pattern worth noticing**, or **Worth a chat** (with a GP, polyclinic or specialist). Care prompts fire on a single concerning entry; logging coverage only gates the pattern levels, never care. The one-line summary follows a written hierarchy (section 3), is never an average, and is replaced entirely while any urgent card is active. No number is shown anywhere in version 2: the regularity estimate proposed in version 1 is deferred until a diary-based metric has been validated (section 5.1). Life stage changes which panels show, which thresholds apply and how the copy reads; age never penalises a level (it only selects care branches that guidelines tie to age) and nothing in the check is rewarded with hearts.

## 2. Why not one number

Established, with the record behind it:

- The app's own evidence register has nothing that validates combining measures into a score. In the jev comb the highest "validates a composite" probability for any of the 49 items was 0.13 (E8, a pain-scale review); every other item was 0.10 or lower. Eight items carry a threshold an app can apply directly (E4, E6, E7, E8, E10, E16, E17, E48); the rest are treatments, remedies or data sources, not inputs.
- The reviewers who shaped this app already took a position. `bio-age.js` returns `score: null` with the note "No validated habit-to-age or composite wellness scoring rule is available" (jev: opposes a composite 0.95, applies to this design 0.87). The Ebb debate dropped the camera "calm score" as arbitrary (0.89, 0.83). The NOVA review asked for a number-free mode because repeated scores can encourage purity rules (0.86). No remark supported a composite (all 0.06 or lower).
- The legacy `cycle-score.js` is the cautionary example: base 70, regularity 40 percent, length in range 25 percent, profile 20 percent, trend 15 percent, clamp 40 to 98, with an estimated score from the profile after fewer than three cycles. It rests on no named evidence (jev 0.03) and lowers the score for being over 40 (0.91). All four personas rejected it; three asked to keep the honest `null` until something is validated.
- Validated sum scores exist, but they are validated instruments with reference standards: the EPDS (Levis 2020, 58 studies, cut-off 11 maximises combined sensitivity and specificity; 13 is more specific) and the HADS, where sum scoring matched latent scoring (Zhang 2026). Rhythme's logs are not such an instrument, and a number without a reference standard would be precision without meaning. Certainty that a composite would be unvalidated: High.
- Prediction from lifestyle is weak even with rich data: machine-learning models on 185 male-partner variables in a preconception cohort reached an AUC of about 0.65 for pregnancy within 6 to 12 cycles (Talaei 2026). A lifestyle number that implied fertility or "cycle health" would overclaim.
- All three reviewers independently asked for the one number that version 1 kept (a diary sleep regularity index) to be dropped or demoted until validated. Version 2 drops it.

Our angle: the useful thing a cycle app can do is show the person the pattern in their own logs against thresholds that clinicians and guidelines actually use, say how much data that rests on, and route the right patterns to a clinician. That is a check, not a score.

## 3. What the rhythm check looks like

- **Where:** the Today card in the room and the You tab (four-week view). The doctor summary (`fourWeekSummary`) gains the same panels with their thresholds and chips, so what the user sees and what the clinician sees agree.
- **Panels:** Rhythm, Body, Day, and Cycle when applicable. Each panel: coverage line ("5 of 7 nights logged; the other 2 are unknown"), up to three observations, level, chips.
- **Levels and words (Psycho method: warm, specific, no judgement, no purity rules; never reassurance for days that were not logged):**
  - Nothing flagged in what you logged: "Nothing in the nights you logged matched the patterns Rhythme checks. The two nights you did not log are unknown."
  - A pattern worth noticing: "Bedtime moved around by two hours or more on several nights. Nothing to fix tonight; it is a pattern worth noticing."
  - Worth a chat: "You logged low mood on five of the last seven days. You deserve support; this is worth talking about with a GP or polyclinic." (the existing MOOD_LOW copy)
  - Not enough days yet: "Log a few more nights and the pattern will show." (shown for pattern levels only; care prompts never wait for this)
- **Chips:** an E number only where the threshold comes from the cited source (E7 for 24 to 38 days, E4 for heavy bleeding signs); a "Rhythme threshold" chip, in a different style, for every product choice in section 6. A product choice never wears an E number.
- **Escalation hierarchy for the one-line summary** (the first that applies wins): 1 an active urgent card replaces the line; 2 Worth a chat from Body for bleeding or pain; 3 Worth a chat from Body for mood; 4 Worth a chat from Rhythm; 5 Worth a chat from Cycle; 6 any "pattern worth noticing", Body before Rhythm before Cycle; 7 "nothing flagged in what you logged"; 8 "not enough days yet". The Day panel never contributes to the line.
- **Number-free mode:** the same panels with counts hidden, as the NOVA review proposed; the user chooses.
- **Never rewarded:** no hearts for any level; the hearts stay with logging and practices.

## 4. Inputs the app already has, and what it needs

Already logged (ovu-store.js TYPES): sleep with bedTime and wakeTime and quality; period flow with clots and impact; symptom with symptoms[] (includes insomnia, hot flushes, low mood, anxiety, irritability, brain fog), pain 0 to 10 and mood; food with tags (home-cooked, veggies, protein, iron-rich, sweet, caffeine, alcohol, ultra-processed, late night), meal time and NOVA group; mindfulness minutes; weight (never used here). Onboarding gives lifeStage, predictionMode, pregState, breastfeeding, periodsBack, plans, hormones, conditions, signals and habits.

Needed before the panels can do what the personas and reviewers asked for:

1. **Time provenance on sleep entries.** When Luna reads "slept 7 h" she assumes a 07:00 wake and back-computes the bedtime (`ovu-orb.js:63`, the entry's note says "Luna assumed a 07:00 wake time"). Those entries would manufacture perfect regularity. Add `assumed: true` to such entries and exclude them from timing metrics (duration still counts).
2. **One night per date.** Sleep is not a single-per-day type, so five sleep entries on one date count as five nights in the existing rules (`ovu-store.js:146`, `ovu-agents.js:37`; reproduced by the Codex review). Aggregate per calendar date before any rule runs, and require adjacent dates for "consecutive".
3. **Night detail:** minutes to fall asleep and number or minutes of night wakings on the sleep entry, and a "broken sleep" mode that accepts several blocks per night (postpartum). Today a 23:00 to 07:00 entry with "awake from 3am" is stored as eight hours.
4. **Hot flush bother:** a 0 to 3 bother rating next to the hot flushes and night sweats symptoms, since bother, not just frequency, is what the treatment evidence measures (Robinson 2026).
5. **Movement:** a movement entry (minutes, kind: walk, strength, yoga, other). Today "I walked for 30 minutes" logs nothing.
6. **LH test and skipped meal entries** (TTC persona; both currently log nothing).
7. **Optional symptoms** for postmenopause (joint aches, vaginal dryness, urinary discomfort) and postpartum (tearfulness, breast pain, wound pain), and a one-time "bone health conversation" question set for postmenopause (section 7).

## 5. Derivation, panel by panel

All rules run on the phone in `ovu-agents.js` style: deterministic, no model calls, every threshold named in the output with its chip. Windows: the last 7 days for levels, the last 14 for trends; both are calendar windows ending today. A day counts as logged for a panel only if it has an entry of that panel's type on that date (after per-date aggregation). Missing days are unknown. Care prompts (section 9) fire from a single entry and from raw text at logging time; they are independent of coverage.

### 5.1 Rhythm panel (sleep timing and regularity)

Eligible night: one aggregated sleep record per date with explicit bedTime and wakeTime (no `assumed` times). Coverage rule for pattern levels: at least 5 eligible nights in 7, and at least 7 in 14 for the spread measure; otherwise "Not enough nights yet". In broken-sleep mode (section 7) timing regularity is not graded; the panel shows total sleep per date, the longest block and the number of blocks.

Descriptive metrics (shown as minutes and hours, never as an index):

- Sleep midpoint per night, in minutes from midnight of the wake day, using the midnight-crossing rule in `sleepHours`.
- **Midpoint spread:** the standard deviation of midpoints over the eligible nights in 14 days. Fischer, Klerman and Phillips 2021 showed in simulations that the standard-deviation family needs more than a week of data for an unbiased estimate and does not capture fragmentation, while day-to-day metrics such as the sleep regularity index are changed by naps and awakenings, which the app does not record (E88). That is why version 2 uses the spread with a 14-day window and drops the index.
- **Bedtime range:** earliest and latest bedtime in the week, shown as times.
- **Social jetlag:** the absolute difference between the mean midpoint on free days and on work days (Wittmann 2006, E56), with Saturday and Sunday as free days by default and a setting to change them; requires at least 2 of each; a weekly measure by construction (E88).
- **Duration:** mean hours, nights under 6 h, consecutive nights under 5 h.

Levels:

- Worth a chat (any one): mean under 6 h over 5 or more nights (existing SLEEP_SHORT_AVG; Rhythme threshold anchored on E10, which recommends 7 h or more on a regular schedule and gives no referral cut-off); 3 or more consecutive nights under 5 h (existing SLEEP_SHORT_RUN; Rhythme threshold); the insomnia symptom on 7 or more of the last 14 logged days together with fatigue or low mood on those days (Rhythme threshold; ICSD-3-TR chronic insomnia is difficulty at least three times a week for at least three months with daytime effects, judged by a clinician, E87, so the copy says "a pattern worth raising", and for perimenopause and postmenopause it adds that CBT for insomnia is the first-line option in current meta-analysis, E67).
- A pattern worth noticing: midpoint spread above 60 minutes, or social jetlag of 60 minutes or more (both Rhythme thresholds; the direction is anchored on the regularity cohorts below).
- Nothing flagged in what you logged: coverage met and none of the above.

What the regularity evidence does and does not say: in 82,391 UK Biobank adults aged 43 to 79, device-measured sleep regularity of 70 or more on the SRI was associated with a 26 percent lower hazard of dementia (Bian 2025, E53), and in 60,977 adults of similar age higher regularity predicted all-cause mortality more strongly than sleep duration did (Windred 2024, E51). Both are observational, device-measured and middle-aged to older; they do not calibrate a diary measure in a 33-year-old or a breastfeeding mother (DeepSeek and Codex both made this point). The menstrual link is direct but thinner: in 2,109 women aged 46 in the 1970 British Cohort Study, the least regular sleep quartile had higher odds of painful periods (OR 1.43), heavy periods (1.34) and PMS (1.33), while sleep duration was not associated with any of them (Abdullayev 2026, E54, cross-sectional, Low, exploratory for other ages); in 3,544 Chinese nurses, rotating shifts were associated with irregular cycles (OR 1.31) and dysmenorrhoea (1.71), with sleep quality mediating only about 7 percent of the shift-work association (Tang 2026, E55, cross-sectional, Low). The panel therefore says regularity is "linked with fewer period symptoms in large studies of women in their forties", never that fixing bedtime will fix periods, and never shows a number.

Contrary evidence kept in view: night-shift work was not associated with anti-Müllerian hormone in 1,641 women (Cromack 2025, E84), so the copy must not imply that irregular sleep harms ovarian reserve.

### 5.2 Body panel (symptoms, pain, mood, bleeding)

Inputs: symptom entries and period entries in the last 14 days (single-per-day types, so one record per date). A day with an entry that reports no symptoms counts as logged and symptom-free; a day with no entry is unknown, never symptom-free.

Care prompts, independent of coverage (any one entry): pain 8 or more on any day (existing PAIN_SEVERE; Rhythme threshold anchored on E8, which supports the 0 to 10 scale, and E9, which says pain that affects daily life is worth a GP visit; neither names 8); bleeding beyond 8 days (existing BLEED_LONG; E7); two or more heavy days with clots or disruption in one period (existing BLEED_HEAVY_IMPACT; Rhythme threshold anchored on E4 and E5, where the definition is impact on quality of life, not a day count); spotting on 2 or more days outside a period in 4 weeks (existing SPOTTING_BETWEEN; Rhythme threshold); any bleeding or spotting after menopause, from any entry type, even once (section 9).

Pattern levels, with coverage of at least 7 logged days in 14:

- Worth a chat: low or bad mood on 5 or more of 7 or more logged days (existing MOOD_LOW; Rhythme threshold); hot flushes or night sweats on 4 or more of 7 logged days with bother 2 or more (Rhythme threshold; NAMS 2023, E24, and the CBT evidence, E66, are what the chat is for); a **premenstrual pattern**: low mood, anxiety or irritability on 3 or more logged days in the 14 days before each of two consecutive logged periods, and, in the week after each period starts (days 4 to 10), at least 3 logged days of which no more than 1 carries those symptoms. The 14-day window matches the RCOG description of PMS (symptoms in the two weeks before the period that improve once it starts; keep a diary for at least two cycles in a row, E85); the counts are Rhythme thresholds; the logged symptom-free follow-up days are required because an absent entry is unknown, not symptom-free. It is a prompt to take the diary to a clinician, not a diagnosis (PMDD screening questionnaires give screen-positive results, not diagnoses, E82).
- A pattern worth noticing: symptoms on more than half of logged days, or mean pain 4 or more, or low or bad mood on 3 or 4 of 7 logged days, or hot flushes on 2 or 3 of 7 days (all Rhythme thresholds).
- Nothing flagged in what you logged: coverage met and none of the above.

### 5.3 Day panel (movement, meals, caffeine, alcohol, calm)

Version 2 gives this panel no level at all. It shows counts with chips and never contributes to the one-line summary; the reviewers were right that a lifestyle "level" would be a grade without a basis. Counts:

- Movement days and minutes in the week, beside the WHO 2020 guideline (E16: 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity a week and muscle-strengthening on 2 or more days). Shown as "3 days with movement, 1 with strength", never as a percentage of target. Why it matters for this app: an integrative review of 55 studies (Hechinger 2026, E68) found the most consistent benefit of activity for menstrual pain and somatic symptoms, weaker and mixed for premenstrual and emotional symptoms, and recommended individual, symptom-responsive activity rather than a cycle-based prescription; a meta-analysis of 29 trials found therapeutic exercise reduced primary dysmenorrhoea severity and pain with low to very low certainty (Arroyo-Fernández 2026, E69).
- Late caffeine: caffeine-tagged food entries with a time within 6 hours of that night's bedtime, counted as nights (Rhythme threshold anchored on Drake 2013, E57: 400 mg taken 6 hours before bed still disrupted sleep in a small crossover trial). The app does not know the dose, so the copy is "caffeine within six hours of bed on 3 nights", never "too much caffeine".
- Alcohol: the existing ALCOHOL_FREQ observation (5 or more tagged meals a week) stays an observation.
- Processed food: the NOVA share with coverage, exactly as shipped (E1 to E3), and the words-only option.
- Late meals: count only. The existing LATE_EATING copy ("often shows up as lighter sleep the same night") should be softened: a 2026 systematic review of nutrition interventions for shift workers found the evidence insufficient for meal-timing guidance (Booker 2026, E79) and a review of dinner timing and sleep is still at protocol stage. Certainty: Very Low.
- Calm practice: minutes and sessions, no target. Contemplative practices improved mood, stress, anxiety and sleep quality in a systematic review of 15 trials in women with PMS, with moderate methodological quality (Walsen 2026, E70); slow breathing (E18).
- Hydration: E17 stays information only; the app does not log drinks.

Stage copy for caffeine and alcohol (each sentence traced to its source in section 7).

### 5.4 Cycle panel (only when periods are expected)

Shown when predictionMode is normal or wide and the person is not postpartum with periods not yet back. It reuses `cycleStats` and the existing flags and adds these rules, all deterministic:

- **Length outside 24 to 38 days** (existing CYCLE_SHORT and CYCLE_LONG, E7).
- **Irregular lengths (FIGO):** over the logged cycles in the last 12 months (the app keeps the last six), the difference between the shortest and the longest exceeds the age band's normal variation: 7 days at ages 26 to 41, 9 days at 18 to 25 and at 42 to 45 (E7; FIGO compares shortest to longest, not consecutive cycles, as the Codex review pointed out; the age bands are as reproduced in the Merck Manual and StatPearls summaries of FIGO 2018, since the full paper was not opened). Requires at least 3 logged cycle lengths. Worth a chat, because irregular cycles are among the conditions for which fertility evaluation should begin without delay (E89) and because menstrual irregularity is a cardiometabolic marker (E72).
- **Transition pattern (STRAW+10):** a difference of 7 days or more between consecutive logged cycles that recurs within 10 cycles (early menopausal transition criterion), or no period for 60 days or more (late transition criterion), from Harlow 2012 (E50). Shown at any age, since STRAW+10 applies regardless of age, body size or lifestyle, with age-framed copy that is a product choice: at 40 and over, "this is the pattern doctors use to describe the menopausal transition; it is not a diagnosis"; under 40, "this pattern is worth discussing: at your age other causes are more common, and an early transition is also possible". Suppressed when the profile reports PCOS, hypothalamic amenorrhoea, hysterectomy or endometrial ablation, or chemotherapy, because STRAW+10 states its menstrual criteria are not applicable to women with PCOS or hypothalamic amenorrhoea, cannot be used after hysterectomy or ablation, and may mislead after cancer treatment (E50). Predictions are already suspended on hormonal contraception and HRT, so the panel is off there.
- **Pregnancy is still possible during the transition.** Whenever the transition pattern shows and the person is not past 12 months without a period, the copy adds: "A long gap is a transition pattern, not a sign that pregnancy is impossible. UK FSRH guidance is to keep using contraception until two years after your last period if you are under 50, and one year if you are over 50." (E92, FSRH 2017 amended 2023, as reproduced in clinical summaries; the primary document was not opened.)
- **Overdue period and pregnancy test:** when the period is overdue past the end of the predicted window and pregnancy is possible, show at once (not at day 45): "You can test from the first day of a missed period, or at least 21 days after unprotected sex if you do not know when your period was due" (NHS, E90). The existing PERIOD_LATE note at 45 days stays as the second reminder.
- **Calendar fertile colouring is off by default.** When the person turns it on from the TTC goal, the band carries this label: "Calendar estimate only. It cannot confirm ovulation, estimate your pregnancy chance, or identify safe days for contraception." (Codex's wording; ASRM says the fertile window is the 6 days ending on ovulation and that calendar prediction has substantial limitations even with regular cycles, E58.) An LH test entry is the better signal and is a prerequisite (section 4).

Why cycle characteristics are worth showing: ACOG treats the menstrual cycle as a vital sign in girls and adolescents (Committee Opinion 651, E73; the framing is extended to adults here as our interpretation), and in 52,655 Nurses' Health Study II women a functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea phenotype in mid-adulthood carried a 62 percent higher cardiovascular risk and non-FHA irregularity was associated with cholesterol, hypertension and diabetes (Shufelt 2026, E72): menstrual history is a health marker worth a conversation, which is exactly the level this panel escalates to.

## 8. What the check never claims, and copy rules

Never: a fertility probability, confirmed ovulation, ovarian reserve or egg quality; contraception protection (the LAM card is information and says pregnancy can occur before the first period; the transition copy says contraception is still needed); a diagnosis of PMS, PMDD, perimenopause, depression or anything else; hormone levels; bone density; biological age; milk supply; treatment suitability; a rating of how good a mother, patient or person someone is. Never a reassuring line while any Worth a chat or urgent state is active, and never "all is well" for days that were not logged. Never "good" or "bad" for food, alcohol or caffeine; counts and the chip only. Never a lower level for age. Never a number.

Copy rules (Psycho method, flexible): say what was logged before what it might mean; one observation per sentence; "worth noticing" for a pattern, "worth a chat" for the clinician level; name the chip; offer the number-free view; the user can untick any entry the rules used.

## 10. Uncertainty, missing days and small data

- Coverage is shown per panel and per window: "5 of 7 nights", "9 of 14 days", with the unknown days named as unknown.
- Below coverage the pattern level says "Not enough days yet" and shows the raw observations only; care prompts never wait.
- Entries with assumed times (section 4) are excluded from timing metrics and labelled in the panel.
- Several entries on one date are aggregated before any rule runs; "consecutive" means adjacent calendar dates.
- Remembered period starts (onboarding recall) give a day count but no phase and no Cycle panel level, exactly as `cycleStats` already treats them.
- Estimated food portions and photo-recognised meals are shown with their "estimate" marker and do not count towards processed-food share when the NOVA coverage rule withholds the percentage.
- Week-to-week comparisons are withheld when coverage differs by more than two days (Rhythme threshold).
- Every observation lists the dates it used and lets the user open and untick the entries.

## 13. Open decisions for Jamie and Dr Patrick

1. **Name.** "Rhythm check" (fits the brand and works without a cycle) or "My wellbeing patterns" (what three personas asked for). Not "cycle wellness score": two of the four stages have no cycle, and "score" promises a number that version 2 does not show.
2. **The product-choice thresholds** in section 6 (midpoint spread 60 min, social jetlag 60 min, insomnia pattern, postpartum exhaustion, hot flush bother, mean pain 4, symptom-day share, premenstrual counts, the two-heavy-day rule, the age framing of the transition copy) need a clinician's sign-off before they ship, and must wear the "Rhythme threshold" chip, never an E number.
3. **New inputs** (time provenance, per-date aggregation, night detail, hot flush bother, movement, LH test, optional symptoms, bone conversation questions) are prerequisites; the Rhythm panel without night detail will mislead the two personas who wake at 3 am.
4. **Safety fixes first.** The urgent-rule gaps in section 9 are more important than any new panel and ship on their own.
5. **Fertile colouring:** off by default with the Codex label when turned on, or removed until an LH entry exists (DeepSeek's position). The design says off by default.
6. **Lifestyle never escalates, has no level and is never rewarded.** Confirm.
7. **If a number is ever wanted**, the honest route is a prospective in-app validation with separate consent (the consent notice already promises research is asked about separately): define the outcome (symptom burden, clinician visits), collect panels for several cycles, and test whether any combination predicts it better than the panels alone. Until then, none.

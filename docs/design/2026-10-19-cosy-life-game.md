Jamie: "My aim of this app is to make it like harvest moon with all the little small details with cute avatar that can
do fishing picking eggs from hen go to mysterious places like hotspring to cook the egg n etc. plant vege and fruits
and watch it grow day by day. Maybe you should study about harvest moon. ... I'm a person who is more than perfection so

is to collect menstrual, sleep and all wellness data points." This page is the study and the suggestions.

## What the sources say

1. **A farm game is a daily loop under an arc.** Harvest Moon's day is "a mini interaction loop": the player assesses
   the farm, decides, acts (water the crops, collect the eggs) and gets feedback (darker soil, a text box); "there is
   only so much time in a single day", so the player chooses between crops and animals; at day's end they update their
   mental model for tomorrow; the arc is the farm's revival, a "content treadmill" that "keeps gameplay fresh while
   never straying too far from its main formula"
   ([Loops and arcs, Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2025/05/14/sketchnote-loops-arcs-lour-drick/)).
2. **Cosiness has three pillars, and known destroyers.** The Project Horseshoe report defines coziness as "how strongly
   a game evokes the fantasy of safety, abundance, and softness": "an absence of danger and risk", activities that are
   voluntary; "a sense of abundance" where basic needs are met so higher needs (relationships, self-actualisation)
   have room; soft, slow, intimate, manageable scope. Its destroyers include extrinsic rewards that create
   "short-term transactional needs", responsibility and mandatory maintenance, notifications, intense stimulus,
   non-consensual social presence, deception, opulence. Its tools: pacing with respite (a bonfire that resets the
   loop), rituals ("repeated, meaningful actions can create familiarity and contentedness"), places built around
   leisure and ritual (cafes, kitchens, gardens) that players decorate; its examples: brewing tea, campfires, cooking
   (Zelda's hot pot, pleasant even when the result is uncertain), farming you may ignore without consequence (Stardew,
   Harvest Moon), shaking trees (Animal Crossing), home decoration. "You can encourage coziness, but you can't force it
   on a player" ([Cozy games, Project Horseshoe 2017](https://www.projecthorseshoe.com/reports/featured/ph17r3.htm);
   [Designing for coziness, Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-for-coziness)).
3. **Retention in farm games comes from unfinished small tasks and unopened doors**, not from pressure: reviews of
   Stardew Valley credit the "one more day" pull to small jobs waiting for the morning and to areas and mechanics not
   yet seen; the same writers warn that chores can turn into tedium and that loops that do not evolve go stale
   (secondary sources only; the creator's own design talks were not found online in this pass).

## What that means for Rhythme

The data is the point and the game is the reward, so the game must be cosy in the report's sense: safe, abundant,
soft, never a chore that competes with logging. Three rules follow.

- **Logging is the morning ritual; the game reads the log back.** The plot already grows with logged days. Everything
  new should grow from the log the same way: the hen lays when sleep was logged, the fish bite on days with a cycle
  note, the hot spring opens on the first full week. Jamie's "give points once they have logged" is right, and the
  cosy framework says keep the hearts as abundance (there is always enough to try things), never as a pressure.
- **Nothing decays, nothing punishes.** Unwatered plants wait; an unfed pet is hungry, not sick; a missed day is a
  quiet day, not a broken streak (the economy note proposes a rest-day token and milestones instead).
- **Every feature is a small ritual with a place.** Each new activity is one tap, a minute, a soft result, in a place
  she walks to (the plot, the hen house, the pond, the hot spring), so the world gets bigger as the rituals add up.

## Suggestions from outside, chosen for a health app

1. **A day review at bedtime.** Harvest Moon's end-of-day update: when she goes to bed, a one-card review of today's
   logs and the hearts earned, and tomorrow's one small thing (the eggs, the seedlings). This is also the natural place
   to ask for the sleep log.
2. **Rituals over chores.** Every activity optional and one tap; nothing requires daily attendance; the plant waits.
3. **Refuge with a threshold.** The home is the refuge; the garden gate is the threshold; the hot spring is the deeper
   refuge earned over a week. Keep intense stimulus out of the home (no notifications inside the scene, soft sounds).
4. **Small unopened doors.** A locked gate behind the garden, a boarded hen house, a dry pond: visible from day one,
   opened by logged days, so there is always one more thing a week away.
5. **Abundance, not scarcity.** Hearts enough for one small thing every week of honest logging; weekly drops add
   choice, never a countdown.
6. **Softness.** Slow pacing, a 24-hour day that matches the real one, the camera close outdoors, the walk cycle, pets
   wandering, sounds of water and birds; no loud wins.
7. **Decorate the refuge.** Arranging exists; add the plot's fence colour, the hen house roof, the bath house tiles.

# A plaza with people in it (19 October 2026, design note, nothing built yet)

Jamie: "I want bigger plaza space to contain more people, can we have different server selection and we can see how many
real people online? I want to create a plaza where people can enter and chit chat, limit the capacity based on each
server channel. User can even create channel with topic they want to discuss. We will have a few standard default and
agents that always standby 24/7 and act like human. The agents name should always rotate and learn to speak like
Singaporean human but not too excessively lah lor hor, just 1 sentence out of 10 have singlish."

This is the first real multiplayer feature, so it gets a design and a review before code. It touches a backend, money,
moderation and the law, and the room so far keeps everything on the phone.

## What exists

- Rhythme keeps every entry on the phone; sign-in (ovu-auth.js) and account grants live in Supabase (project
  ovaria-app), with Edge Functions for podcasts and Plus. The plaza today shows four sample neighbours (Mei, Nadia,
  Priya, Sofia) from `ovu-community.js`, with a noticeboard of local notes, a tea stall, a fountain and two benches.
- Under-13 profiles exist (the onboarding splits them) and must never enter a chat.

## Shape of the feature

1. **Servers are plazas.** Three to start, named like Singapore places (Orchard, Tiong Bahru, Punggol), each a Realtime
   channel with presence. The plaza sheet lists them with "12 people here · 3 hosts", and the room's plaza scene joins
   one. Capacity is per plaza (24) and per topic channel (12); joining asks an Edge Function that checks the live count
   (presence plus a `plaza_members` heartbeat table) before handing back a short-lived channel token, so a full plaza
   refuses politely ("Orchard is full, Tiong Bahru has room").
2. **Real people and hosts are counted separately.** The hosts are labelled as hosts (a small badge on the nametag and
   in the count). They may be warm and human in tone, but a person must never be misled into thinking a host is a
   person: that is the line between a friendly presence and deception, and health conversations are exactly where it
   matters. The count Jamie asked for ("how many real people online") is the people count, with hosts shown beside it.
3. **Chat.** Short messages in the plaza (speech bubbles over avatars and a scrolling strip at the bottom), stored in
   `plaza_messages` for 24 hours, signed-in accounts only, rate-limited by the Edge Function (one message a second,
   120 an hour), with a word list, a report button, mute and block, and the house rules on first entry. No photos.
   Under-13 profiles see the plaza but not the chat. Nothing a person types leaves the plaza into Luna or the journal.
4. **Topic channels.** Anyone signed in can open a channel with a title and a topic (for example "Perimenopause chat",
   "PCOS and food"), capacity 12, listed under its plaza, closed after 24 hours without a message. The creator can close
   it; reports go to Jamie's review list.
5. **Hosts.** A worker runs round the clock (an Edge Function on a schedule, or a fleet agent calling the same
   function) and keeps at least two hosts in every default plaza. Each host has a name from a rotating pool of
   Singaporean names, a short persona (where she lives, what she does, a thing she likes), and a voice guide: warm,
   plain English, short sentences, curious about the other person, never medical advice ("ask your doctor" where it
   matters), never pretending to have a body or a cycle of her own, and light Singlish: at most one sentence in ten
   carries a particle or a local turn of phrase ("can", "lah", "shiok", "makan"), never stacked, and never in a
   sentence about health. Names rotate at the start of each host's shift (four to six hours) and a name is not reused
   within a week. Hosts reply only when someone speaks and go quiet after two unanswered messages. A host's messages
   are generated by the fleet's model with the persona and the last twenty messages of the channel as context; a
   second pass checks each reply against the rules (no medical advice, no claims to be human, Singlish ratio) before
   it is posted.
6. **Presence on the plate.** Phase one shows people as the existing neighbour sprites (a pool of twelve looks) with
   nametags, placed on the plaza's stand points and walking to a new spot when they speak. Phase two broadcasts live
   positions at two updates a second so people see each other walk. A bigger plaza plate (two screens wide, the camera
   scrolls) comes with phase two, so the space grows with the crowd.

## What has to be true before it ships

- **Safety.** House rules; report, mute and block; no under-13 in chat; no private messages in phase one; retention 24
  hours; Jamie can close a plaza or a channel from the admin. Hosts labelled. A clear line in the plaza: "Hosts are
  here to keep company, not to give health advice."
- **Privacy (PDPA).** The only personal data is the display name and what a person types; the privacy notes gain a
  plaza section; export and delete cover plaza messages.
- **Cost.** Supabase Realtime on the current plan: concurrent connections and messages per month; with capacity 24 per
  plaza and three plazas the ceiling is 72 people plus hosts, well within the free tier; the host worker's model calls
  are the real cost (about one call per reply) and are capped per hour.
- **Tests.** Core: capacity maths, the Singlish ratio checker, name rotation, channel expiry. Smoke: the plaza sheet
  lists servers with counts (mocked Realtime), joining a full plaza refuses, an under-13 profile sees no chat.

## Phases

1. Presence and counts: join a plaza, see people and hosts counted, no chat yet. (Supabase presence, Edge Function
   join, plaza sheet.)
2. Chat in the default plazas with the rules above, one default host per plaza.
3. Topic channels, host rotation and the voice guide with its checker, reports admin.
4. Live walking of others and the wide plaza plate.

Phase one is a few days; the full set is two to three weeks of evenings. The host voice needs a model call each
reply; the choice of model and its monthly cap is Jamie's.

## Phase 1 built (19 October 2026, branch `claude/plaza-servers`)

Jamie: "I want to make sure the channel server works and data can flows to our login dashboard."

- **Server** (`supabase/migrations/20261019080000_plaza_presence.sql` and `20261019090000_plaza_presence_v2.sql`, both applied the same day): three
  plazas (Orchard, Tiong Bahru, Punggol, room for 24 each) in `plaza_servers`; one row per signed-in account in
  `plaza_presence` while she is in a plaza; `staff.plaza_daily` keeps joins and the peak per Singapore day with no
  account ids. Calls: `plaza_list`, `plaza_join` (since v2 every call first takes one global advisory lock and sweeps
  quiet rows, so capacity holds and two joins cannot deadlock; a full plaza answers with one that has room), `plaza_beat` (every 25 s; after 75 s of quiet she is no longer
  counted and must join again, which checks capacity again), `plaza_leave(p_server)` (removes only the named plaza). All four need a signed-in account; the
  tables are closed to the app's roles. Staff read counts through `admin_plaza`, audited like every other staff call.
- **Phone** (`ovu-plaza.js`): a pill in the plaza ("Choose a plaza · 34 online", then "Orchard · 8 people here"), a
  chooser sheet with each plaza's count and Join, Full or You're here, and a Leave button. Walking out of the plaza,
  putting the app away, going to sleep or signing out takes her out; her choice is remembered and she rejoins when
  she walks back in. Signed out, nothing is sent; the pill says "Plazas · sign in to join".
- **Dashboard** (`admin.js`): a Plazas card on Today with people in each plaza now, the peak and joins today, and a
  14-day table, refreshed every 20 seconds.
- **Hosts and chat** are phases 2 and 3; nothing here talks or pretends to be a person. The sample neighbours stay
  labelled as samples.

Verified: a rolled-back transaction on the live database with 26 synthetic accounts (24 fill Orchard, the 25th is
refused and pointed to Tiong Bahru, a move frees a seat, a quiet phone is asked to rejoin, Leave removes the row,
signed-out callers are refused on every call and on the table, a non-staff caller of the staff call is refused and
audited, the owner sees counts only); live calls with the publishable key alone are refused (401); the core suite
(plaza words) and the smoke suite (signed-out pill; a signed-in run against a fake plaza server: count, chooser,
join, full refusal, check-in, Leave, walking out and back, sleep; the staff card) pass.

### Review record (Codex, two rounds, 19 October)

Round one asked for changes; the second cut (migration v2, the reconciling client, the disclosure, the stale staff
card) resolved the deadlock, cleanup, named leave, sign-out leave for a settled membership, focus, staff staleness and
ownership. Round two still found, and this is where the work stops for Jamie's decision (AGENTS gate 6, a second
failed review on the same lifecycle finding):

- signing out while a join is on its way leaves her counted until the 75 s lapse;
- the same account on two phones in different plazas keeps moving itself between them every check-in;
- a pagehide leave can, after a back/forward-cache restore, remove a newer visit to the same plaza (self-heals on the
  next check-in);
- a stalled token refresh or keepalive leave can leave the phone's loop waiting with no deadline (a reload clears it);
- the day's peak is sampled by joins and check-ins, so people who all leave just after midnight are missed;
- the staff card's first request blocks the other dashboard timers until it settles, and an older reply can overwrite
  a newer one;
- the notice does not say how long the daily join and peak counts (no account attached) are kept.

The proposed fix for the next round: a visit id issued by `plaza_join` and required by beat and leave (a lease), so a
late or duplicate request can only touch its own visit and a displaced phone stops instead of reclaiming; one
departure path for hide, pagehide, sleep and sign-out; one deadline around token, request and keepalive; the staff
card on its own timer with ordered replies; daily counts kept for a year and said so.

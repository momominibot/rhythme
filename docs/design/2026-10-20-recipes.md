# Recipes for PCOS, menopause and everyday eating, and the Mediterranean check

20 Oct 2026. Jamie asked for "good recipes from the internet that help with PCOS, menopausal and ovarian health", and
then called the old Mediterranean score page an ugly interface. Both now live in the room.

## What it is

- **A Recipes folder** on the Learn desk, and two rows in the Nutrition folder. It opens a sheet with three filters
  (PCOS (PMOS), Menopause, Everyday), each with a short note on what food can and cannot do, recipe cards, two
  HealthHub guides for eating in Singapore, a pregnancy food-safety line, a care line, and its sources.
- **Each card links to the source's own page** and keeps its own title. We add one line about what is in the dish.
  Nothing is copied: no ingredients, no steps, no photos.
- **The Mediterranean check**, in place of `medas14.html` (now a redirect stub to `room.html#medas`). It asks 13 of
  the 14 MEDAS questions with Yes or Not usually, keeps the answers on the phone (`state.medas`, through
  `OvuStore.commit`, which puts the store back if a save fails), shows "x of 13" with no grades, and offers recipes
  for each "not usually".
- **Desk folders** without a saved spot now take the first free place of a grid that fits the wallpaper. The old
  default put a third row at 96%, so the ninth folder already sat below the edge, and a tenth would have been hidden.
  A drag saves the whole desk; if a folder added later has no free place on her arrangement, the whole desk goes back
  on the grid with a note (Jamie, 21 Oct). Folders open on a click, so Enter and Space work.

## Sources, each opened on 20 Oct 2026

Recipes (title, link and ingredients checked against each page or PDF):

- **Jean Hailes for Women's Health**, the Australian women's health charity. Its PCOS recipes page, reviewed by its
  clinicians on 21 May 2026, links eight recipes; six are used for PCOS. Its menopause article links the savoury
  phytoestrogen bread. Other recipes are from its recipe index: tofu and sesame stir-fry, fish and vegetable kebabs,
  roasted carrots with linseed dukkah, and "Power-me-up granola" (the page slug says iron-rich granola).
- **Bone Health & Osteoporosis Foundation** (US, formerly the National Osteoporosis Foundation), whose bone-healthy
  recipes page lists 27 PDF recipes. Used: spinach and ricotta cannelloni, gingered salmon croquettes (canned salmon
  with its bones), cod with tomatoes and black olives, chunky spicy hummus, four-bean salad, and bean and tuna salad.
- **HealthHub (HPB)**: My Healthy Plate ("Quarter Quarter Half") and Healthy Hawker Food.

## The Mediterranean check, adapted

- Items as published (Table 1), in plain words with the same thresholds and serving sizes. The old page got item 13
  wrong (it asked about whole-grain bread; the real item is choosing chicken, turkey or rabbit over red and processed
  meat) and narrowed item 11.
- **Item 8 is left out.** It gives a point for drinking 7 or more glasses of wine a week. The sheet says why: nobody
  should start drinking for health, and the NHS advises no alcohol when trying to get pregnant.
- Without it, this is not the validated 14-point score, and the intro says so. It also says the study was in older
  Spanish adults at high heart risk, that the links are associations, that it was not built for fertility or the
  ovaries, that nobody needs every habit, and to skip it if keeping track of food makes you anxious.
- Two hints keep fidelity and add honesty: 4 tablespoons of olive oil "is the level in the Spanish study, not a goal
  for everyone", and juice counts "in the original checklist", though whole fruit is the better everyday pick.

## Reviews and what we did

Codex read-only (with web search), DeepSeek V4 flash and Doubao Seed 2.1 pro (as a Singapore dietitian and
gynaecologist panel). Records are in `docs/debate/2026-10-20-recipes-*.md`.

Taken:
- **Codex:** "makes enough for the week" invited seven-day leftovers (SFA advises 3 to 4 days), so it now says ten
  servings. "Food does not stop hot flushes" overstated the evidence, so it now says no food or diet change has been
  shown to ease them. The portion and frequency rules ("a weekend dish", "a small bowl") are gone. The sweets item is
  restored to the published wording (custard included), and rabbit is back in item 12. Saving goes through
  `OvuStore.commit` with a visible message on failure (it also stopped aliasing the stored answers). The selected Yes
  is now `#3a7559` (white text 5.4:1; it was 2.8:1). The filters are `aria-pressed` buttons that keep focus. Desk
  spots search further and never pick an occupied cell. A first visit through the old link now opens the check after
  the welcome instead of losing it.
- **DeepSeek and Doubao:** the precise MEDAS associations, "an association, not proof", and "not that score". "Not
  usually" instead of "Not yet" (no implied progression), and "Ideas, if you would like them". Calcium "when the
  tofu's ingredients list calcium sulphate". Eating the soft bones of sardines and salmon. "Some or all of the white
  rice". Plant iron, and seeing a doctor for heavy periods. Unsweetened kopi-o or teh-o kosong. A pregnancy
  food-safety line (NHS, E84), and one care line for allergens, kidney disease, diabetes, thyroid conditions and past
  eating disorders. A PCOS line on seeing a doctor, where checks of blood sugar and heart health belong. HealthHub's
  "Lower-Calorie Hawker Foods" was swapped for its neutral "Healthy Hawker Food" page.

Not taken, with reasons:
- **"PMOS is not an accepted rename"** (both models): it is. The Lancet published the consensus in June 2026 (E83),
  and Jean Hailes, AAFP, AJMC and others report it; a transition period is under way, so both names are in use. The note says so.
- **Narrowing item 7 to sugar-sweetened drinks** (DeepSeek): the published item counts carbonated drinks too.
- **Changing item 1 to "olive oil or another unsaturated oil"** (Doubao): that would change the instrument. The hint on
  item 2 covers the Spanish context instead.
- **"The 200 g shellfish serving needs checking"** (Doubao): Table 1 says exactly that.
- **Per-card pregnancy warnings** (both): one pregnancy line under every list says it once, without crowding the cards.
- **A folic acid dose** (DeepSeek: 400 mcg): left out in round one because the page we had open gave no dose. Codex
  round two pointed to the NHS pregnancy vitamins page, which states it, so the dose went in (see below).

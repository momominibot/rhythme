What is STRAW+10?

The STRAW+10 (Stages of Reproductive Aging Workshop +10) is the international gold standard for staging female reproductive aging. Originally published in 2001 and updated in 2012 with a decade of additional evidence, it provides a structured framework spanning from menarche to late postmenopause.

The system divides a woman's reproductive lifespan into three broader phases — reproductive, menopausal transition, and postmenopause — with sub-stages numbered from -5 (early menarche) to +2 (late postmenopause). Each stage is characterised by specific menstrual patterns, hormonal profiles, and clinical features.

📋 Why It Matters

STRAW+10 gives clinicians and researchers a common language to describe where a woman is on her reproductive aging journey. It moves beyond chronological age alone and uses actual physiological markers — cycle length, flow, FSH, AMH — to stage the process. StageLabelTypical AgeKey Characteristics
-5 Early reproductive 9–15 First menstrual period; cycles often anovulatory for 1–2 years
-4 Peak reproductive 14–18 Establishing regular ovulatory cycles; HPO axis maturing
-3a Late reproductive a 19–28 Most regular cycles; peak fertility; optimal ovarian function
-3b Late reproductive b 29–38 Slight cycle shortening (24–27 days); AMH begins decline
-2 Early menopausal transition 39–43 Cycle length variability (>7 days); FSH 10–15 on CD3; AMH < 1.5
-1 Late menopausal transition 42–47 Persistent ≥7-day cycle difference; FSH 15–25; night sweats begin
+1a Early postmenopause 45–50 ≥60 days amenorrhea; FHS > 25; erratic estrogen
+1b Mid postmenopause 48–53 12 months no period; FSH > 25; hot flushes peak
+1c Late postmenopause 53–60 Vasomotor symptoms stabilise; vaginal dryness; bone density decline
+2 Late Postmenopause 60+ Full estrogen depletion; highest CVD and osteoporosis risk
Note: Ages are population averages. Individual trajectories vary significantly.
Applying STRAW+10 to Your Health

Knowing your STRAW+10 stage helps you and your healthcare provider make proactive decisions:

  • Stage -3a to -2: Focus on fertility awareness, cycle tracking, and building bone density and muscle mass
  • Stage -1: Monitor cycle variability; test FSH and AMH annually; consider egg freezing if family building is deferred
  • Stage +1a: Discuss hormone therapy options with your doctor; begin bone density and cardiovascular baseline screening
  • Stage +2: Prioritise strength training, protein intake, and cardiovascular health; continue bone density monitoring

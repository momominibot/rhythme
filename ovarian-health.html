What your records can tell you

Dates, symptoms and test results can help you notice patterns and prepare questions for care. They do not add up to an ovarian age, egg-quality score or personal fertility probability.

Early menstrual years

Keep dates, flow, pain and the effects on school or daily activities. Patterns in the first years after periods begin need adolescent context. An adult fertility rule cannot substitute for that assessment.

Trying to conceive

Mucus, waking temperature and urine LH are different observations. They can support a fertility-awareness or clinical conversation, but an entered positive LH result is not an Rhythme finding that ovulation occurred. Keep the test brand, timing and instructions with the result.

After pregnancy

Bleeding after birth is not automatically the start of a menstrual cycle. Keep postpartum and breastfeeding context with entries and discuss recovery concerns with your care team. Do not use absent periods as an app assurance against pregnancy.

Perimenopause

Track changing bleeding patterns and symptoms, including their effect on sleep or daily life. Review medicines and lab collection context together. Rhythme does not diagnose menopause from age, one FSH value or a count of days.

After menopause: bleeding deserves care

Arrange medical assessment for any vaginal bleeding after menopause, even slight spotting or a single episode. Recording it does not replace assessment. If bleeding is heavy or you feel faint or seriously unwell, seek urgent care.

Connect the record to a useful question

Bleeding, pain and dates

Use the timeline to explain what changed and what disrupted your day. It cannot identify the cause of bleeding or prove ovulation. Include symptoms and treatment rather than assuming every bleed is a new cycle.

Record observations

Temperature, mucus, LH and PdG

Ask how method, illness, medicines and timing affect interpretation. Waking thermometer readings differ from wearable estimates; urine concentrations differ from app line ratios. Keep the original output. Rhythme does not turn a single result into ovulation confirmation or a fertile-window prediction.

AMH, AFC, FSH and other lab results

AMH and AFC can help clinicians assess reserve and plan ovarian stimulation. Reserve concerns egg quantity, not egg quality. These tests do not alone establish the chance of natural conception. Keep dates, original units, assay and treatment context to review with your clinician.

Mood, sleep, symptoms and habits

Describe what you noticed and how it affected you. Two entries occurring together are a question to explore, not proof that one caused the other. Rhythme does not turn sleep, pulse, stress or food notes into ovarian-age or hormone scores.

Prepare for your next conversation

  1. Choose the dates and changes you want to discuss.
  2. Include relevant medicines, contraception, pregnancy or postpartum context.
  3. Bring original lab reports or test instructions alongside entries.
  4. Ask what further assessment, if any, fits your situation.
